- **M2 (in progress): room mapping and saved rooms.**
  - `RoomMappingFlow` in the session's Space step:
    - a known room loads silently;
    - a room the platform already knows is saved automatically;
    - an unmapped room gets a briefing with an auto-start countdown, then Space Setup (Quest) or a guided look-around, then an automatic save.

- **ImmersiveX Media** (`com.immersivex.media`, [guide](docs/media.md)): one **Immersive Media** component plays any supported media in the room.
  - **Detection and players:** it detects the format and attaches the right player:
    - GenXR 3.5D hologram streams;
    - Gaussian splats: 3DGS `.ply`, compressed `.ply`, `.splat`, `.spz` v1–v4 and `.ksplat` levels 0–2;
    - point clouds;
    - glTF/GLB models with animation, through glTFast 6.20.0;
    - OBJ and PLY meshes;
    - frame sequences of any of these (a sequence `.json` or a folder);
    - flat, 180° and 360° video, mono or stereo.
  - **The same acrylic controls on every format:**
    - play/pause, seek, time, speaker and volume;
    - greyed out for stills;
    - the panel follows the media's position but not its rotation.
  - **Side handles:** a move bar under the controls, turn bars at the sides (about the up axis only) and a resize corner. They work with hands or controllers, by ray or pinch.
  - **Several pieces of media per scene:**
    - the first stands at the centre of the mapped room and the rest around it, each facing the user;
    - each stays where it was put, held by a spatial anchor (see M3 below).
  - **Rendering:**
    - splats of every format go through one URP splat shader (maths matched to GenXR's web player), sorted on worker threads;
    - big scenes keep their most visible splats (400,000 on standalone headsets);
    - meshes draw unlit;
    - 360° video draws on an inside-out sphere, with per-eye stereo halves.
  - **Sound:** Android MediaPlayer on Meta Quest (`IStreamAudioProvider`). It's the clock for streams and sequences.
  - **Vendor formats:** `IMediaCodec` is a plug-in point for them (4DViews, Arcturus), which need their own SDKs.
  - **Samples:** `tools/samples/make_media_samples.py` generates a 5 MB test sample of every format (git-ignored, not committed), all showing one asymmetric test figure. EditMode tests decode each one and check its orientation; they're skipped when the samples haven't been made.
  - **Demo scene:** **ImmersiveX ▸ Demos** builds a streamed-hologram demo scene, the first scene in the build. Bundled content such as a 4D capture is added locally with Add Media; it isn't committed.
  - **Bundled 4D content:**
    - A 4D Gaussian-splat capture (a folder of 3DGS `.ply` frames) is packed into a hologram stream that ships inside the app and plays offline, at 17 bytes a Gaussian (now by Add Media; see D4).
    - A stream's `stream.json` can carry one `fit` for the whole clip, so a character keeps its size when it raises an arm.
    - The generated content (`Assets/StreamingAssets/ImmersiveXContent/`) is git-ignored because of its size.
  - **Automation:**
    - `media list`, and `media [@n|@name] status|play|pause|toggle|seek|mute|unmute|speaker|volume|turn|move|height|open`;
    - `demo`;
    - `resolve` (re-resolve packages).
- `WalkableArea.Centre()` and `FloorGrabTransformer` (slide across the floor, turn about the up axis only) in core.

- **Content from the internet, with no code (D4).** Checked against 88 real files from Khronos, PlayCanvas, Niantic, BabylonJS, GaussianSplats3D, Open3D, Stanford, 8i, three.js, videojs, Wikimedia Commons and others, in XR Simulation and the Meta XR Simulator. 20 EditMode tests decode them when the local corpus (`research/web-corpus`, git-ignored) is present.
  - **ImmersiveX ▸ Media ▸ Add Media…** (and automation `media add`): a file, a folder of frames or a URL is added to the open scene as Immersive Media.
    - Local content is copied into `StreamingAssets/ImmersiveXContent/<name>/` with the files it needs: an OBJ's MTL and textures, a glTF's buffers and images, a PLY's texture.
    - A folder of splat frames is packed into a hologram stream. The packer runs in the editor, reuses the player's decoders, handles every splat format and replaces the Python converter; its output is identical.
  - **Build check:** a build stops when a scene's Immersive Media content isn't in StreamingAssets, has missing frames, or points at a path on this computer. Plain http gets a warning. **ImmersiveX ▸ Media ▸ Check Content in the Build** and `media check` run it on demand.
  - **Photos:** `.jpg`/`.png`, flat on a screen (PNG transparency kept) or 360°/180° around the user, mono or stereo.
  - **Stereo setting** on Immersive Media. Once a video or photo is known to be 360° or 180°, its layout is inferred from its shape: a square 360° frame is top-bottom, a 2:1 180° frame is side-by-side (VR180).
  - **glTF compression:** Draco meshes, `EXT_meshopt_compression` and KTX2 (Basis Universal) textures, through Draco for Unity 5.4.3, KTX for Unity 3.7.0 and Unity's meshopt decompression 0.2.0-exp.1.
  - **Splat scans:** a scan's far background (an outdoor capture's sky shell) is left out, so the capture stands in the room instead of drawing as a bubble.
  - **Unsupported formats say what to do:** HDR/EXR/HEIC/WebP, FBX/USDZ/Blend, PCD/LAS, SOG, splatv and MKV/AVI each get a conversion hint. A video that won't decode names the codecs that do.
  - **Meshes:**
    - OBJ with several materials: one submesh each, with its MTL colour and texture; texture options and file names with spaces work.
    - `.stl`, binary and ASCII, z-up.
    - Parts with only a colour, or none, are lit, so plain models show their shape; textures and vertex colours stay unlit.
  - **Folder sources everywhere:** a folder of numbered frames plays as a sequence on the headset too, because Android builds get an index of StreamingAssets folders. A folder holding `stream.json` or `sequence.json` plays that file. A **Frame Rate** setting covers captures that don't say.

- **M3 (in progress): content stays put with spatial anchors.** It moves only when the user moves it.
  - `ContentAnchor`, added by Immersive Media and Immersive Content:
    - at start-up it restores the saved spatial anchor, else the pose saved with the room;
    - when the user lets go it saves the pose at once, then 0.5 s later anchors it there, saves the anchor and erases the old one;
    - first placements are anchored too;
    - content follows its anchor through tracking corrections and headset off/on, and hides while the anchor is lost.
  - Anchor saves, loads and erases run one at a time, and a failed save is retried once.
  - `PlacementIndex` (one JSON file per room, version 2): pose relative to the floor, size, anchor id and save time. Version-1 files still load. With no mapped room, placements are saved relative to the tracking space.
  - "Forget room" erases the room's saved anchors.
  - Automation's `media turn|move|height` save the placement as a release would.

- **Interaction rules (D5).**
  - **The ray only clicks** (`DirectGrabOnly`, `ContentLayer`, `ContentClickRaycaster`): a hand or controller at the content picks it up; rays, gaze, teleport and climb never grab. Content bodies sit on Ignore Raycast, so the ray never blocks a panel, and rays click 3D parts.
  - **Handles are controls** (`ContentHandles`, shared by Immersive Media and Immersive Content): move bar, turn bars, a new **turn ring** around the base, and a resize corner, by ray or hand. Make Immersive objects get them too, and their size is saved with their anchor.
  - **Panels win near them** (`PanelZones`): with a hand at a panel, nearby grabbables and handles let its buttons take the press.
  - **Hands have no pointer** (`HandPointers`): tracked hands press and grab within reach; controllers keep their rays. Settings ▸ Hand Pointers gives hands rays.
- **Selectable parts** (`ContentParts`, `PartLabel`): models with several meshes, Unity objects (opt-in), web scenes and 4D meshes. A click selects a part (again for the whole), a hand picks it up, **Put back** / **Put all back** return it. Parts move only while the content is paused; solid colliders, and none for a scene's ground. A far part's label comes within reach of the user.
- **Content menu** (`ContentMenu`, `HandSwipes`, `SwipeDetector`, `SwipeGate`): lists the scene's content and shows one piece at a time (remembered), with **Quest menu** and **Close app**. A quick sideways swipe towards the body's middle opens it in front of the user (right hand right→left, left hand left→right); the reverse closes it. Claps and the hand's way back don't count (0.8 s cooldown). Settings ▸ Content Menu.
- **Web experiences in 3D (D6).** A three.js page (`.html`, a folder with `index.html`, or a URL) plays as Immersive Media:
  - it runs out of sight in a web view: headless Chrome in the editor, Android WebView on Quest (`OffscreenWebView`), with app content served locally;
  - a JS bridge (`WebBridge.js`) streams its scene, which `WebScene` rebuilds natively (PBR, instanced, skinned and line meshes); actors become parts;
  - the page's own controls, captions and cards show on a panel beside it;
  - one placement rule for every page, applied once when it has loaded: its ground is the floor, what its camera looks at stands at the content's place; what sinks below the floor is hidden;
  - hidden by the menu, the page holds its clocks, frames and sound and resumes when shown.
- **4D splat packing rules (D8):** one fit per clip from medians (centre and height) and the ground the clip comes down to (90th percentile of its lowest points); splats more than 10× the clip's height away are left out (`SplatCloud.Within`).
- **MR and VR modes (D14):** the content menu's header has MR and VR buttons, each with its icon (drawn in code by `ModeIcons`); every start is in MR. VR (`ViewModes`) turns the passthrough off (`SeeThrough.Remove`), gives the Quest its boundary back (`IBoundaryProvider.RequestShow`), and puts the user in `VirtualWorld`: a low-lit night (deep blue sky with faint stars, a dusky horizon haze, a dark floor on the real one with a faint metre grid and a soft pool of light where content stands, melting into the haze), so any content stands out. Content keeps its place in the real room, and `RoomGuard` shows the real walls (from the mapped floor outline, up to the ceiling) and furniture (Space Setup's boxes) as a soft grid when the user comes within about a metre, with a faint line along the floor always. Both shaders live in core's Resources. `RoomFloor` (the floor height and the room's middle) moved from Immersive Media into core so both use it. Automation: `invoke vr|mr|view`.
- **The content menu opens at start (D14):** in front of the user as soon as the session is ready.
- **Content starts in front of the user (D16):** when the app starts and whenever a piece is picked in the content menu, Immersive Media and Immersive Content (*In Front Of User*) stand in front of the user, on the floor under them and facing them, then stay put, anchored, until the next start or pick; earlier visits' anchors aren't restored. With the content menu, the piece showing takes the spot in front. A mapped room counts only when its floor is under the user (`RoomFloor.IsUnder`: 0.4 to 2.3 m below the eyes), so a room mapped on another storey no longer puts content at that storey's floor, keeps it inside that room's outline, or draws its walls in VR. Immersive Media's *Placement* (Room Centre / In Front Of User) is gone.
- **Space in the content menu (D15):** a **Space** button in the footer (a green dot when the room is saved, amber when not) opens `SpacePanel` in the menu's place: whether the room is saved, its floor, walls, ceiling and when it was saved, with **Re-create space** (maps the room again, on Quest through Space Setup; the menu comes back when it's done) and **Back**. VR mode redraws the room's walls when a re-created room is saved. Automation: `contentmenu space|back|recreate`.
- **Large 4D meshes (D13):** containers of several hundred megabytes play on a Quest (a four-minute scene of 7,231 frames, 614,000 triangles and 303 MB loads in about 4 s and runs at 72 fps). Containers load from a file: inside an APK they're copied to the cache once per build (`MediaSource.FetchToFile`), and `Mesh4DContainer.ReadFile` unpacks a gzipped one straight into a buffer of its final size (the size its gzip trailer gives), so loading needs about the container's own size in memory, not three or four copies.
- **Bare metal content (D14):** an app that owns everything (its menus, layout and what it shows) with ImmersiveX only as its host. [docs/bare-metal.md](docs/bare-metal.md) covers it.
  - `BareMetalContent` on the app's object, and `IBareMetalApp.OnHostReady` when the headset is ready. `SessionPlan` decides what the session sets up: for bare metal, permissions, the see-through view, a hidden boundary, hands and controllers, and nothing else (no content menu, room-mapping prompt, placing or MR/VR switch unless the app asks). Nothing ties the app to a mapped room.
  - `ImmersiveHost`: the user (head, floor, hands, the content menu's swipes), world ray casts against depth (Quest 3 and 3S, through `XR_META_environment_raycast`), the room's planes and furniture and the app's colliders, `MapRoom` on request, `HostAnchors` and `KeepInPlace`, the view mode, the boundary, the system menu, a data folder.
  - The headset's camera (`IPassthroughCameraProvider`, `HostCamera`, `CameraFrame`): pictures read back without stalling a frame, each with its camera pose and intrinsics, and rays through any point of a picture. Quest 3 and 3S: the Passthrough Camera API (`MetaQuestPassthroughCamera`, lens poses converted as Meta's samples do, `LensPose`). Editor: a simulated camera that sees the app's own scene.
  - Quest builds get `android.permission.CAMERA` and `horizonos.permission.HEADSET_CAMERA` only when a build scene's bare metal content reads the cameras (`MetaQuestCameraManifest`, `BareMetalBuild`); other apps lose the camera permission Unity adds for `WebCamTexture`.
  - **ImmersiveX ▸ New Bare Metal Scene**, and **Host Check** (`Assets/Demos/BareMetal`), a small bare metal app with a button for each host feature.
  - `ImmersiveXSession.MapRoom()` maps the room on request, and returns whether it started.
  - Automation: `host status|camera|capture|raycast|target|anchors|map|view|swipe|press` in Play mode, `host scene` in Edit mode. 8 EditMode tests.
- **JWT Guitar Performance:** a second GenXR 3.5D hologram stream in the demo scene's content menu, after 3.5D Xperience: 24 seconds of a guitarist and guitar cut out of single-camera footage, streamed from `genxr-streaming-media` (`demos/JWT/stream-v3.json`, base quality), 1.3 m tall as a seated player.
- **No app-data backup:** Quest builds set `android:allowBackup="false"` (`MetaQuestStoreManifest`), which clears the Meta Horizon Store's security vulnerability warning "Application allows backup of private data". Nothing ImmersiveX saves is worth restoring on another headset: placements are held by spatial anchors in this one's room.
- **App icon:** the ImmersiveX logo (`Assets/Branding/Logo.png`) is the app's icon in the headset's app library. `MetaQuestBuildConfigurator` sets it as the default icon and the Android square and round launcher icons on every build (not as an adaptive icon, which is cropped to its centre and would cut off the wordmark); Validate warns when it's missing.
- **Replay switch:** the media controls have a Replay switch between the time and the speaker. On, the media starts again each time it ends; off, it stops at the end. It starts as **Loop** is set (`ImmersiveMedia.Replay`, `SetReplay`, also in `IMediaTransport`), and media loaded with Loop stops when it comes round to the start once it's switched off. Automation: `media replay [on|off]`; `media status` reports the switch.
- **Content menu scrolls (D13):** when there's more content than its six rows, up and down buttons on the right of the list scroll it a row a click (faded at the ends), with a track showing where the rows are; it replaces the page arrows. Opening the menu brings the content that's showing into view. Automation: `contentmenu scroll up|down [rows]`.
- **The hologram look for 4D meshes (D12):** SP4M version 4 (`look: "hologram"`): every object drawn as light computed per pixel by the new `ImmersiveX/Mesh 4D Hologram` shader (rim, contour lines in its own frame, scan band, scanlines, flicker, materialise front, the web player's maths), added over the room; per frame its glow, how far it has materialised and a global fade; one mesh instead of an atlas; effects as baked Gaussians.
- **Controls within reach (D11):** every media's controls stay with the user, low on their right at elbow level (`WithinReach`), instead of standing by the media. The panel keeps still while the user looks around, looks at it, leans in or drags a slider, and glides after them when they walk, sit, stand or turn away. It's 36 cm wide with the media's name on a tab; the move bar under it moves the media from there. Captions stay with the media, on the user's side. Automation: `walk <right> <forward> <seconds>`; `media status` reports the panel and caption positions.
- **4D meshes, version 3 and following the subject (D10):** `Mesh4DContainer` reads SP4M version 3 beside version 2: scenes coloured per vertex (every object a one-member family, with a per-vertex variant that runs the rules), each object's own cutaway (plane or wedge, a level per frame), colour keyframes at listed times decoded on demand (a full copy every 64), a sphere dissolve with the scene's own noise, `follow`, `stage.footprint` and `stage.roomHeight`. The object table has 8 rows per object. `Mesh4DPlayable` follows a subject that leaves: it stays at the media's place at its size while the set dissolves at a radius or sinks below the floor (version 2 content gets it from its subject track). Add Media sets **Height** from `stage.roomHeight`; standing controls stand clear of a wide footprint and never further out than the user.
- **4D meshes (`.sp4m`, D9):** `Mesh4DContainer`, `Mesh4DRenderer`, `Mesh4DPlayable`, `Mesh4DParts`. Atlas meshes and engine families from one object table, colour keys painted on the GPU, cutaway, door and dissolve rules in the shader, baked effect Gaussians through the hologram renderer, captions and callout cards through `IMediaAnnotations` and `MediaCallouts`. Add Media renames a `.sp4m.gz` to `.sp4m` and the build check refuses `.gz` names (Android's packer unzips them).
- **Meta Horizon Store packaging:** `MetaQuestStoreManifest` and the configurator make every Quest build follow Meta's release manifest rules (install location auto, landscape, excluded from recents, Meta's device identifiers, default device targets `quest2+`). Release signing steps in the Quest guide.
- **Docs:** `CONTENT_GUIDE.md` (every content type and the structure it needs); README, media and Quest guides brought up to date.
- Automation: `media scene|remove|first`, `media … parts|inspect|node|click|js`, `contentmenu status|open|close|select|move|swipe`, `parts …`, `invoke pointers|reach|uirays`.

### Fixed
- 4D mesh parts followed object 0 when they had no hull (`List.Find` returns 0 when nothing matches), and a recorded scale read as the user moving a part. Together they drew a whole 4D scene bigger round its first object once that shrank, throwing other objects towards the user. Each part now follows its own object, and its transform has no scale.
- Soundtracks bundled in the APK were silent on Quest: MediaPlayer can't open `jar:` URLs, so they now open through `AssetManager.openFd`.
- Play buttons on Quest ignored fingers and pinches when a long grab box or the move bar was right under the panel; panel glass also blocked rays.
- Web pages re-measured while racing through their load and froze on the first press; they're now placed once, after loading. A page's huge ground no longer gets a collider that picked up the whole base.
- The old 4D clip fit used a mean centre and a median floor, which put large scenes far away or sank them; see the packing rules above.
- SPZ v3/v4 files with more than about 60,000 splats crashed the app. The decoder allocated 16 bytes of stack per splat and overflowed the worker thread's stack, which Mono reports as SIGILL; that includes Quest. It was found with PlayCanvas's `biker.spz`.
- Splat renderers released their GPU buffers while still drawing that frame; they now stop drawing first. Video does the same with its texture.
- An OBJ with a material library but no `usemtl` drew untextured; it uses the library's material again.
- Automation's `media open` takes a quoted source with spaces.
- The automation bridge could read `command.txt` between its creation and its first write, and drop the command. It now waits for content; writers should write `command.tmp` and rename it.
- Shapes created at runtime (anchor marker, panel grab bar, walkable-area outline) rendered pink on device. They now use a bundled URP Unlit material (`ImmersiveXRuntime.mat`), referenced from the settings asset so it's always in the build.
- The walkable-area outline was drawn in the wrong place on Quest. On see-through and room-aware platforms the session now zeroes the rig's camera height offset, so the camera and the room's planes share one space. The outline follows the XR Origin's trackables.
- XR Simulation showed a flat yellow background. The baseline now adds AR Foundation's **AR Background** URP renderer feature.
- Play mode waited 10 s for head tracking in XR Simulation. Tracking is now also confirmed by `ARSession.state`.
- In the editor, Play mode waited for the Game view to have focus before placing content, which stalled automation runs. Only devices wait for focus now.
- Quest passthrough didn't draw. The configurator now enables the Meta Quest OpenXR feature set, including its required Composition Layers Support, and `Validate()` reports if it's off.
- Quest wasn't detected on device. `MetaQuestAdapter` now recognises the running Meta session and logs why when it doesn't.
- Room scanning no longer starts on platforms without room data.
- Meta's start-up boundary request (which failed with `XR_ERROR_HANDLE_INVALID`) is off; ImmersiveX hides the boundary once passthrough is showing.
- The starter scene's camera is saved with a transparent clear and HDR off.
- Quest builds use native render pass and GPU skinning, as in the Quest-proven VizionEnterprise project.
